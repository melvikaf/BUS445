Cell2Cell | Data cleaning and shared split

We used the supplied Cell2Cell CSV, a cross-sectional record of customer usage, billing, service, equipment, and retention history. The file contains 51,047 customers and 58 fields, including 14,711 recorded churners (28.8%). The case dictionary describes approximately 71,000 customers. The supplied file defines the analysis sample. The binary churn outcome has no verified prediction horizon, so its sample rate cannot be read as monthly churn.

The business question is which customers to review first when the retention team has limited outreach capacity. Cleaning keeps the recorded values and their units traceable across model comparisons.

The churn label describes the recorded observation period. In the raw CSV, Yes means the customer left and No means they stayed during that period.

Cleaning rules

Item What happened in the shared dataset Why
Blank fields and literal NA tokens Set to R’s NA when reading the source CSV through na.strings = c("", "NA"). They represent unavailable values.
HandsetPrice Set all 28,982 cells containing the literal text Unknown to R’s NA, then converted the column to numeric USD. The resulting missing prices are numeric NA values. Recorded prices can now be summarized and modeled as amounts.
CurrentEquipmentDays Replaced negative values with NA. Elapsed time with a handset must be zero or positive.
Zero household ages Flagged for review and kept as recorded. The meaning of zero is unverified, and age is outside the current predictor sets.
Negative monthly revenue Flagged for review and kept in USD. Signed amounts may reflect billing adjustments.
Scaling and clipping Kept original units. No z-score standardization, min–max normalization, or outlier clipping was applied. The current unpenalized logistic models can fit predictors in different units. Original units make revenue, tenure, and usage easier to interpret.
Customer records Checked that customer IDs were unique and that every row had a valid churn label. Kept all rows. Records with missing predictors can still contribute valid data.

What this means

Before cleaning, HandsetPrice had 0 cells counted as missing because the source encoded 28,982 unavailable prices as the literal text Unknown. R counted that text as a recorded value. After replacing each Unknown with R’s NA, the missing-price count rose to 28,982. Those prices were already unavailable in the source data. Cleaning made that explicit.

For CurrentEquipmentDays, the missing count rose from 1 to 77 because 76 negative values were replaced with NA. Together, these two changes account for the increase from 3,515 to 32,573 cells counted as missing.

Where missing values are filled

R stores missing values as NA. The export uses write.csv(..., na = ""), so those cells appear as empty fields in the shared CSV. EDA reports missingness where applicable. In 03_Variable_Selection.Rmd, each cross-validation fit fills missing numeric predictors with the median of observed values in that fold’s fitting rows. For example, a missing handset price in a validation row gets the median recorded price from the fitting rows. Missing categorical predictors use the most frequent category in the fitting rows. These temporary replacements apply to both fitting and validation data and stay inside the modeling step. This prevents validation data from influencing imputation.

Seasonality and calendar dates

The case dictionary describes one cross-sectional record per customer. The CSV has no observation date, billing period, or churn event date. The quarter-over-quarter change fields show the direction and size of a change but do not identify calendar quarters. MonthsInService measures elapsed tenure. A change by tenure cannot be assigned to November or Black Friday. Testing seasonality would require dated customer billing or usage records spanning multiple periods. Daily or weekly dates would be needed to isolate Black Friday from the rest of November.

Shared training, test, and cross-validation assignments

We use a 75/25 split stratified by churn and three stratified CV folds within training, with seed 445 for each assignment. This preserves the assignments used in the preliminary analysis. The test partition has a blank CV fold. For fold 1, the model fits on folds 2 and 3 and scores fold 1. Each training fold serves as validation once. The test partition is reserved for final evaluation after the team locks the model and predictors.
